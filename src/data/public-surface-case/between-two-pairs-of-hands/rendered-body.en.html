Elections / Child Protection / Institutional Handoffs

Between Two Pairs of Hands

An election-eve rally, a family waiting for answers, and what happens between one hand letting go and another taking hold.

Case date26 November 2026
Source cutoff26 November 2026

01

Solomon’s judgment

In the biblical account of Solomon’s judgment, two women claim the same child. When Solomon orders the child divided, one woman asks him to let the other take the child alive. [1]

On November 25, Taipei mayoral candidate Puma Shen (Shen Po-yang) cancelled his election-eve rally on Ren’ai Road, citing traffic disruption. The stage came down overnight. The decision drew a public comparison to the woman who yielded in Solomon’s judgment. It also left open the question of how the city had allocated its streets. [2, 3]

02

What the families asked for

Four days before the rally was cancelled, parents in a Taipei case involving alleged mistreatment at a preschool approached Shen outside a televised policy forum. They called for rules on how long preschool camera footage should be kept and how families could request access to it. They asked for help pursuing legal accountability after the election. [4]

The city said a November 27 mediation session would go ahead. The session concerned a dispute over refunds. Administrative and criminal proceedings were separate, and the questions the parents raised about access to evidence and support for their children extended beyond the mediation. [4, 5]

03

Who takes the next step

In December 2023, a child known as Kai Kai died after being placed with a home-based childminder in Taipei. The Control Yuan examined how the placement was arranged and how Taipei’s social welfare authorities followed up on his care, including through visits. [8]

In a separate preschool sexual abuse case, the Control Yuan censured the city over its handling of the first report. It found that the city failed to carry out the required administrative investigation or track the case, and that its education and social welfare offices failed to coordinate their response. [9]

In September 2026, Shen had proposed that city departments share information and review serious incidents to identify ways to prevent harm to children. The November pledge set out steps for the families who had approached him. If elected, he said he would convene a cross-departmental team on his first day in office to help preserve evidence, provide information and arrange counselling for children and parents. [4, 6]

Taipei already has procedures for handling child-protection cases across agencies. An additional team would have to show what changes at the point where one office hands a case to another. Who can secure a recording before it is lost? Who confirms that a referral has been received? Who keeps the family informed while the investigation is underway? A department can finish its part while the family is still waiting for the next one to begin. [7]

At night, there is the faint tap of cloven hooves in the dark. Something is looking for a newborn. Someone can ask what happened where no one was looking. Someone can ask what would stop it from happening again.

As the world shook and the music rose, there was nowhere left to retreat. Still, the child passed from hand to hand, toward a tomorrow no one could see.
