## Why this exists

The discipline it describes is already implemented in three places, at three
different granularities:

```text
public-slice            boundary               what one fact does not establish
human-governed-...      "does not establish"   what a page does not claim
AGENT_WORKLOG.md        Unresolved questions   what a round did not settle
```

Three separate implementations of one rule are present, but none states the
rule in one place. This draft states the rule explicitly.

It describes how claims are handled rather than asserting that any of them are
right. The statement therefore addresses claim handling separately from the
evidentiary status of the material it governs.

## The statement

> This system distinguishes between claims that can be checked and accounts
> whose underlying record is not public.
>
> Where a claim is presented as checkable, its assumptions, limits, and cited
> record are stated beside it. Where the underlying record is private or
> unavailable, the account is identified as not independently verifiable and is
> not assigned the same evidentiary status.
>
> This is a statement about claim handling, not about the correctness of any
> finding.

## How a reader can check it

The examples that follow are the checkable part of the statement. They apply to
material whose record is public; they do not extend to accounts whose record is
private, and those accounts are marked as such where they appear.

```text
1  AGENT_WORKLOG.md carries "Unresolved questions" and "Risks or assumptions"
   as standing fields, not as occasional remarks. Count them against the
   number of entries.

2  Superseded material is present, not deleted. Version folders are additive;
   a retraction appears as a new entry that names the old one, and the old one
   is still readable.

3  The public pages carry explicit "does not establish" lists. Read those
   before the claims they qualify.
```

If any of those three stops being true, the conditions recorded here no longer
hold.
